How to turn an exhibition booth into qualified leads, not footfall.

At FFCC KSA 2026 in Riyadh, Magnaite's booth registered 600+ visitors, and 70% of them qualified as real leads. Here is the method, step by step.

By Reem Dissoky · Published 30 October 2026 · Updated 30 October 2026

How do you get qualified leads from an exhibition?

Decide who you are there for before you design anything. Build the booth to prove that person's problem. Run the floor yourself. Follow up while the conversation is still warm.

Most booths are built to be seen. Big logo, big screen, free coffee. They get footfall. Footfall is not a lead.

Step 1: Write the audience thesis

Before the booth, before the brochure, one sentence: who will be in that room, and what are they carrying?

For Magnaite at FFCC KSA 2026, it was multi-branch F&B owners carrying a cost they don't have a name for: one slow second at the counter, at the drive-thru window, repeated across every branch. Not "F&B professionals". That specific owner, with that specific pain, standing in one building for two days.

If you can't write that sentence, don't book the space yet.

Step 2: Design the booth to prove the cost

Not to describe the product. Every element of the Magnaite booth was there to make the slow second visible: the layout, the installation, the on-screen content, the brochure, the giveaways. A visitor who has that problem recognises it in seconds. A visitor who doesn't, walks on. Both outcomes are good.

Step 3: Decide what "qualified" means before the doors open

Registrations are easy to inflate. Pick the criteria in advance: the right role, the right business size, the right problem. Then count against them. At FFCC, 70% of the 600+ registrations met the bar. That ratio was the number the whole participation was bet on.

Step 4: Run the floor like it's the sales call

I ran the booth myself for the full event: demos, conversations, notes. The person who wrote the thesis is the best person to test it live. You hear which line lands, which objection repeats, which visitor is serious. That's research you can't buy afterwards.

Step 5: Follow up while it's warm

A registration is a promise to continue a conversation. The follow-up should pick up exactly where that conversation stopped, not send a generic brochure a week later.

Step 6: Capture content on the floor

Shoot while you're there. At FFCC, an unscripted video from the event floor outperformed the professionally produced pieces on TikTok. The room is content. Use it.

What happened at FFCC KSA 2026

2,000+ visitors at the event. 600+ registrations at the booth. 70% qualified. And Magnaite signed Drive Coffee on stage, for systems integration across its branches. The room I said would be there was there. Read the full case.

Questions people ask

What is a good lead qualification rate at an exhibition?

It depends on your criteria, which is why you set them before the event. At FFCC KSA 2026, Magnaite's booth qualified 70% of 600+ registrations against criteria set in advance.

Should the marketing lead staff the booth?

If they wrote the audience thesis, yes. They can test it live, adjust the pitch, and come back with insight no report will give them.

What should an exhibition booth focus on?

Proving the specific problem your ideal visitor has, not describing your product. The right visitor should recognise themselves in seconds.
