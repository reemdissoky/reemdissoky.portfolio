HR Tech · SaaS · Saudi Arabia

Mawred HR

An AI-integrated HR and payroll platform for the Saudi market. Social content translating a genuinely technical product — attendance, payroll runs, employee requests, performance reviews — into content non-technical decision-makers actually engage with.

mawredhr.com ↗

What was the brief on Mawred HR?

The brief was to make a technical HR and payroll platform make sense to the people who decide whether to buy it.

Mawred HR is an AI-integrated platform built for the Saudi market. It handles attendance, payroll runs, employee requests and performance reviews. Each of those is a real workflow with real detail behind it.

The people reading the content were not the engineers who built it. They were business owners, managers and finance leads. They care about what the system saves them, not how it works underneath.

I ran this account during my time as Digital Marketing Manager at Ether Tech, managing Saudi brands from Egypt. The content was written in Arabic, for a Saudi audience.

Why does HR software need translating at all?

Because the features are not the reason anyone buys.

A product team describes a payroll module by what it calculates. A decision-maker hears that and still has to work out what it means for their month. That gap is where most software content loses people.

So my job was to close it. I took each feature and asked one question: what problem does this remove from someone's week?

  • Attendance becomes fewer disputes and less time checking records by hand.
  • Payroll runs become a process that finishes on time without a scramble at the end of the month.
  • Employee requests become fewer messages chasing approvals.
  • Performance reviews become a record a manager can actually refer back to.

Same product. Different entry point.

How did I approach the Arabic content?

I wrote it to be read by a manager, not a developer.

Arabic tech content often borrows English terms or translates them word for word. The result reads as foreign, or as heavier than it needs to be. I kept the language plain and close to how Saudi business owners talk about their own operations.

Technical terms stayed where the audience already uses them. Everywhere else, I chose the everyday word.

The aim was simple. Someone scrolling past should understand the point of a post in one read.

What does this kind of work look like as a service?

It is a mix of copywriting in English and Arabic and social media strategy, applied to a product that is hard to explain.

I start with the product itself. I need to understand what each part does before I can decide what to say about it. Then I map features to the outcomes a buyer would recognise. Then I plan content that carries those outcomes, one idea per piece.

On accounts like this, a supporting team handles design under my supervision. I write the copy, set the direction, and check everything before it goes out.

HR as a topic also connects to employer branding, which I cover separately. If you want the thinking behind that, I wrote about employer branding for small companies.

Clear beats clever when the product is complex.
