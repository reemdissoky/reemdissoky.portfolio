AI · B2B Tech · Dubai, UAE · 2026 · Ongoing

Appana Technologies

A Dubai AI company that builds and integrates AI agents and small language models for businesses, from on-premise enterprise AI to a UAE-hosted monthly plan for SMEs, alongside playful consumer apps. Two audiences, one brand.

The website. A full audit of appana.ae: structure, messaging, and how clearly each product explains itself to the person it's built for. Delivered as a set of recommendations.

LinkedIn, B2B. A strategy for reaching the business decision-makers behind AI adoption in the UAE: who to reach, what to say to them, and how often. In progress.

appana.ae ↗

What does Appana actually sell?

Appana sells specialist AI to businesses, in three forms, plus consumer apps on the side.

  • ACI Enterprise. Specialist AI that runs on-premise or in a private cloud.
  • ACI Sovern. Security and governance for AI agents.
  • ACI Flow. A UAE-hosted monthly AI plan for smaller companies.

The tagline is "Bold ideas. Connected intelligence." The company was founded by a UAE national.

That is a wide range for one brand. An IT director weighing private infrastructure, a compliance lead worried about what agents can touch, and a business owner who wants a monthly plan are three different readers. Each one arrives with a different question.

That range is where the work starts.

Why start with a website audit?

Because the website is where every other channel sends people. If a LinkedIn post earns a click and the page that follows doesn't answer the reader's question, the post did its job and the site lost the lead.

So the audit looked at the basics first. Is the structure clear? Does the messaging hold up? Does each product explain itself to the person it is built for, in their terms, not the engineer's?

The output was a set of recommendations Appana can act on.

What does a B2B LinkedIn strategy for AI need to answer?

It needs to answer three things before anything gets posted: who to reach, what to say to them, and how often.

AI is a crowded conversation on LinkedIn. Most of it is broad and sounds the same. A company selling on-premise AI and agent governance has something more specific to say, and the people who need to hear it are a small, findable group.

My approach is to write for that group directly. Fewer, clearer posts that a decision-maker can forward internally without explaining them. Product language translated into the business problem it solves.

The strategy is in progress. I will add to this page as it moves.

Where do the consumer apps fit?

They sit beside the business products, not inside them. That is the "two audiences, one brand" problem in practice.

Playful consumer apps and enterprise AI security can share a name. They cannot share a tone on every channel. A buyer reviewing governance for AI agents needs to see a serious company first. Someone downloading an app needs to feel the opposite.

The way I approach it is to keep the two from blurring. The business story leads on LinkedIn and on the pages a decision-maker reads. The consumer side keeps its own voice where its own audience is.

One brand. Two clear entry points.

Who is this kind of work for?

Technical companies with a real product and a message that hasn't caught up to it. It suits teams who want one person to read the site, set the direction on LinkedIn, and own both. More in Brand Communication and Social Media.
