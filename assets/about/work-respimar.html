Health & Wellness · Kuwait

Respimar

A natural nasal-spray line for the Kuwaiti market — decongestant, cleaning and hydration, and pediatric formulas. Content built around one throughline, "safe for you, safe for them" (آمن عليك وعليهم), carried through ingredient education, lifestyle content, and pediatric-specific messaging.

@respimar_kuwait ↗

What was Respimar's content built on?

One line: "safe for you, safe for them" (آمن عليك وعليهم).

Respimar is a natural nasal-spray line in Kuwait. The range covers a decongestant, a cleaning and hydration spray, and pediatric formulas. Three different products, three different moments of use.

Without a shared idea, a range like that turns into three separate conversations. The feed starts to look like a product catalogue. I wanted every post to point back to the same promise, so the brand reads as one thing.

Why "safe for you, safe for them"?

Because safety is the first question people ask about anything they put in their body, and the second question is whether they can give it to their children.

In a family-centred market like Kuwait, a health purchase is rarely made for one person. A parent buying a nasal spray is often thinking about the whole household. The line speaks to both at once: the adult using it, and the people they care for.

It also works in Arabic as a natural phrase. آمن عليك وعليهم sounds like something a person would say, not a slogan written for an ad.

That matters. A throughline only works if it can be repeated without wearing thin.

How did one line carry three kinds of content?

Each content stream answered the same promise from a different angle.

  • Ingredient education explained what is in the sprays and why a natural formula matters. This is where "safe" earns its meaning.
  • Lifestyle content placed the products in everyday moments, so the brand felt like part of normal life and not only a remedy.
  • Pediatric messaging spoke directly to parents. This is where "safe for them" does most of its work.

The topics changed. The promise stayed the same.

How do I approach health and wellness content?

Carefully, and with the facts the brand can stand behind.

Health content has a higher bar than most categories. People are deciding what to trust with their own bodies and their children's. Overclaiming does real damage, both to the reader and to the brand.

So I start with what the product actually is and what it is made of. Then I look for the one human concern that sits underneath the category. For Respimar, that concern was safety for the family. Everything else is built on top of it.

I write the strategy and the copy myself, in Arabic and English. On accounts like this, design is handled by a supporting team under my supervision, and nothing goes out without a quality check against the plan.

If you want to see how I build a campaign idea from a single line, my creative campaign ideas page explains the process. For the writing side, see copywriting.

A good throughline is one the audience could say back to you.

Who is this kind of work for?

Health, wellness and family products with more than one item in the range.

A single promise keeps the range together and makes each new product easier to introduce.

It keeps the brand consistent. Each product gets its own content, but every piece supports the same promise, so the audience remembers one idea instead of three.
