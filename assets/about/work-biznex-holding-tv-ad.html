Scriptwriting & Voiceover Direction

BiznEX Holding — TV Ad

A TV campaign linking BiznEX's services to the universal entrepreneurial dream of an ongoing business — a promise of limitless guidance along the investment journey.

What was the idea behind the ad?

The idea was to connect BiznEX's services to something nearly everyone wants: a business that keeps going.

Not a big exit. Not a single deal. An ongoing business, one that lasts.

From there, BiznEX's role became a promise of limitless guidance along the investment journey. The viewer is the one with the dream. BiznEX is the support that stays with them through it.

Why build a TV ad on a dream instead of a service list?

Because a service list asks the viewer to do the work, and a dream does the work for them.

TV is a short, passing medium. Few people remember a list of services from a TV spot. They remember how it made them feel about their own plans. If the ad gets that right, the services have somewhere to land.

The dream of an ongoing business is also broad enough to include almost everyone watching. Someone already running a company, someone saving to start one, someone who has thought about it for years. Each of them can see themselves in it.

That is what a mass medium needs.

Why does this idea fit an Egyptian business audience?

Because for many people, a business is a family plan, not just a financial one.

A shop, a workshop or a company is often something people hope to keep running for years, and sometimes to pass on. The word "ongoing" speaks to that hope directly. It is less about growth for its own sake and more about staying power.

Guidance fits the same hope. Starting is one decision. Keeping a business alive takes many, over a long time. A promise of support along the whole journey answers the worry people carry quietly: what happens after the first step?

The ad's job was to name that worry gently, and answer it.

What did I do on it?

Two things: I wrote the script, and I directed the voiceover.

The script set the idea, the structure and every line. The voiceover direction made sure the delivery carried what the script meant, not just what it said.

Why does voiceover direction matter so much?

Because in a TV ad, the voice is often the brand.

The same line can sound like a promise or like a sales pitch, depending on pace, warmth and where the weight falls. A script about long-term guidance needs a voice that sounds patient and sure, not rushed.

Directing a session means listening for that, take after take, and knowing when a read is right. The writer is usually the best person to hear when a line has drifted from its meaning.

How does this connect to the rest of BiznEX?

It is part of the same body of work.

I also worked on the brand rebuild and the "Find Your Business Match" campaign for BiznEX, and on the brand writing, website copy and social media for the Egypt Retail Summit, which BiznEX Holding created with retail experts.

Who is this kind of work for?

Brands that need a broadcast or video script with one clear idea at its centre, and someone to protect that idea through production.
