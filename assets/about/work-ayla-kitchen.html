Campaign Creative & Copywriting · London, UK

Ayla Kitchen

Naming built on the Arabic word "Ayla" (family) — humanizing the brand around the feeling of being cared for. Full playful, pun-driven campaign built on top of it — "One Bite, Habibi!", "Falafel it up, Habibi!" — across product ads and social grids.

Where did the name Ayla come from?

Ayla is the Arabic word for family, and I chose it to make the brand feel like being cared for.

Food is one of the most personal things a brand can sell. People remember the meals someone made for them more than the meals they bought. A name that means family puts the brand on that side of the line: closer to a home kitchen than a counter.

For a brand in London, the name also does something quieter. It carries an Arabic word into a city where many people will hear it for the first time. That gives the brand a small story to tell every time someone asks what it means.

Why a playful, pun-driven campaign?

Because warmth and humour sit naturally together, and a name about family needed a voice that felt friendly, not formal.

The campaign lines did that work: "One Bite, Habibi!" and "Falafel it up, Habibi!"

"Habibi" is a word many people recognise even outside the Arab world. It is affectionate, casual and instantly familiar. Pairing it with English food puns made the lines easy to read for a London audience while keeping the Arabic character of the brand front and centre.

A pun also makes a line memorable. People repeat what makes them smile.

How did the campaign carry across formats?

It ran across product ads and social grids, with the same voice in both.

Product ads put a single item and a single line together. The pun does the selling, and the product does the rest. Social grids work differently. They are seen as a set, so the voice has to hold across many posts at once without feeling repetitive.

A strong naming idea makes that easier. When the name already means family, every line can lean into warmth and playfulness without having to explain itself.

How do I approach naming and campaign copy?

I start with the feeling the brand should leave behind, then find a word that holds it.

Naming is not about sounding clever. It is about choosing a word the brand can build on for years. Ayla works because the meaning gives the copy somewhere to go.

From there I write the campaign lines in English and Arabic, test them against the brand's voice, and check that each one could stand alone on a poster or a post. On projects like this, design is usually handled by a supporting team under my supervision.

You can read more on copywriting, creative campaign ideas and brand communication.

A name should give the copy somewhere to go.

Who is this kind of work for?

It suits food and hospitality brands that want a name and a voice before they want a media plan.

If you are launching a new kitchen, a product line or a restaurant concept, the name and the first campaign set the tone for everything after. Getting them right early is cheaper than fixing them later.

It also suits brands with Arab roots selling to audiences outside the region. The question there is how much of the original language to keep, and how to make it feel welcoming rather than foreign. Ayla answers that with one word and a friendly "Habibi".

Keep the meaning. Make it easy to say.

Questions people ask

What does Ayla mean?

Ayla is the Arabic word for family. The name was chosen to humanise the brand around the feeling of being cared for.

What were the campaign lines for Ayla Kitchen?

"One Bite, Habibi!" and "Falafel it up, Habibi!" They ran across product ads and social grids.

Can you write bilingual copy for a brand outside the Arab world?

Yes. Ayla Kitchen is based in London. The copy kept its Arabic character while staying easy to read for an English-speaking audience.

Do you do naming as a standalone service?

Naming is part of my brand communication work. Other naming projects include eLiver and Everybear Agency.
