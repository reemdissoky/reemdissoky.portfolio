Dental Education · MENA · Project-based, agency

Perfect Braces Academy

Social media planning and content calendar for a British-affiliated orthodontics training program for practicing dentists across the Arab world — course promotion, MORTH exam-prep milestones, and real graduate certifications used as social proof.

perfectbracesacademy.com ↗

Who was Perfect Braces Academy talking to?

Practicing dentists across the Arab world who want to train in orthodontics.

Perfect Braces Academy runs a British-affiliated orthodontics training program. Its audience is already qualified, already working, and already busy. They are not looking for a career. They are looking for the next step in one they have.

That shapes everything about the content. A dentist deciding on further training weighs time away from the clinic, cost, and whether the qualification will mean something to their patients and peers. The social media had to speak to those concerns directly.

The work was project-based, done agency-side. I handled social media planning and the content calendar.

What went into the content calendar?

Three streams: course promotion, MORTH exam-prep milestones, and real graduate certifications used as social proof.

  • Course promotion told prospective students what the program offers and when to join.
  • MORTH exam-prep milestones marked the stages on the way to the exam, which gave the calendar a natural rhythm tied to the exam path.
  • Graduate certifications showed real dentists who had completed the program.

Each stream had a different job. Together they covered the full path from interest to enrolment.

Why use real graduates as social proof?

Because for professionals, peers are the most credible source there is.

A dentist considering a program wants to know that people like them have done it and come out with something real. A certification from a working dentist answers that question better than any claim the academy could make about itself.

It also keeps the content honest. Every certification post points to a real outcome, not a promise.

Why plan around exam milestones?

Because the academic calendar is already the calendar the audience lives by.

Students preparing for an exam think in terms of dates and stages. Planning content around those same milestones means the academy shows up at the moments that matter to its students. It also gives the calendar structure, so planning is not guesswork from one month to the next.

How do I approach education and professional training brands?

I start with what the learner is risking and what they hope to gain.

Professional audiences are sceptical by habit. They respond to clarity, evidence and respect for their time. So I keep the content specific: what the course covers, what the milestones are, who has finished it.

I build the strategy and plan the calendar myself. On accounts like this, design is handled by a supporting team under my supervision, and everything is checked against the plan before it goes out.

For more on how I plan accounts, see social media and how I work.

Proof from peers carries more weight than any headline.

Who is this kind of work for?

It suits training providers, academies and certification programs whose audience is already working in their field.

These audiences do not need to be convinced that learning matters. They need to know that this program is worth their time and that it leads somewhere real. A planned calendar with clear milestones and real graduates answers both.

Plan for the learner's path, not the launch date.
