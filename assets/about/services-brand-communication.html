Strategy

Brand Communication

Positioning and messaging that gives a brand a consistent point of view across every channel it shows up in — from a campaign concept down to a single caption.

I don't start with channels. I start with the position — the one sentence that has to be true before anything else makes sense. BiznEX needed to move from "an expo with almost no brand history" to "the recovery every entrepreneur was owed" — that shift is what let a tomorrow-facing slogan, three named zones, and a full visual identity all agree with each other instead of reading as separate decisions. Magnaite needed a different kind of consistency: a systems-integration brand that has to read as trustworthy in a two-minute booth conversation and in a LinkedIn caption, using the same handful of words each time.

See it in: BiznEXSee it in: Magnaite

What's included in brand communication work?

A position, the words that carry it, and the rules that keep every channel saying the same thing.

  • The position. One sentence that has to be true about the brand before anything else is written.
  • Messaging. The handful of phrases the brand repeats, in English and Arabic, and the ones it avoids.
  • Audience thinking. Who the brand is talking to, and the moment it reaches them. For Magnaite at FFCC KSA 2026, that was multi-branch F&B owners and "one slow second" at the counter or drive-thru.
  • Tone by channel. How the same position sounds in a booth conversation, a LinkedIn caption, a TikTok clip and a website headline.
  • Applied work. The campaign concept, the copy and the content that put the position in front of people.

The position comes first. The rest is built on it.

How do you work on it?

I write the position and the messaging myself. That part doesn't get delegated.

I start by listening: to the founders, to sales, and where I can, to customers. At Goot Resorts I sat in on sales calls and visited the resort and its competitors as a guest. A brand's real message often shows up in what people say on the phone, not in the brief.

Once the position is agreed, a supervised team handles design and media buying against it. I check what they produce against the position before it goes out. If a visual or a caption says something the brand doesn't mean, it doesn't ship.

We agree up front what we'll measure, so the conversation later is about numbers, not taste.

Who is brand communication for?

It suits a few situations more than others.

  • A brand that's growing faster than its message, where each channel has started saying something slightly different.
  • A company entering a new market, where the old way of describing itself doesn't land with the new audience.
  • A technical product that decision-makers don't fully understand. Mawred HR needed its HR and payroll platform explained in Arabic for non-technical buyers.
  • A launch or relaunch, like BiznEX after 2020, where the brand has little history and needs a clear story fast.

What do you get first?

A written position and a short messaging set you can test before anything is designed.

It's cheaper to change a sentence than a campaign. So we settle the words, check them against real conversations, and only then move into channels.

See it applied in Respimar, where one throughline, "safe for you, safe for them", carried ingredient education, lifestyle and pediatric content.

Questions people ask

What is brand communication?

Brand communication is how a brand explains itself, consistently, across every place it appears: campaigns, social, the website, sales conversations and events.

Do you write brand messaging in Arabic?

Yes. I write in English and Arabic myself, and decide per audience which language leads.

How is this different from a brand identity?

Identity is how a brand looks. Brand communication is what it says and how it says it. They need to agree, so I set the message first and design follows it.
