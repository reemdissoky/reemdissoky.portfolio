Concept & Creative Writing · Cairo, Egypt · Proposed — not implemented

Cairo Dental Arch

Concept creation for a dental exhibition — merging the architectural idea of a curved gateway with the dental term for an upper or lower denture, framing the event as a passageway for dentists. Agency-side proposal; not built.

Was Cairo Dental Arch built?

No. Cairo Dental Arch 2021 is a proposed concept that was not implemented.

I developed it agency-side as a pitch for a dental exhibition in Cairo. What follows is the thinking behind the proposal, not the record of a live event. There are no results to report, because the concept was never run.

I include it here because the idea itself shows how I work: finding one word that can carry an entire event.

What is the idea behind the name?

The word "arch" means two things, and the concept uses both.

In architecture, an arch is a curved gateway. You walk through it to get somewhere. In dentistry, an arch is the upper or lower denture, the curved line of the teeth. Every dentist uses the word daily.

Putting the two meanings together framed the exhibition as a passageway for dentists. The event becomes a gateway: a place you pass through on the way to the next stage of your practice.

It also gave the event a point of view. The proposal positioned Cairo Dental Arch as "a curved point of view" compared with other dental exhibitions. The curve is in the name, in the teeth and in the idea of seeing the field from a different angle.

Why build an exhibition concept on a double meaning?

Because the audience gets the second meaning instantly, and that makes the name feel like it was made for them.

Dental exhibitions speak to a specialist audience. A name that only a dentist would fully understand creates a small moment of recognition. That moment is what makes a name stick.

A double meaning also gives the creative team plenty to work with. The arch can become the entrance, the stage, the visual identity and the copy. One idea, used everywhere.

What would the proposal have included?

The proposal covered concept creation and creative writing: the name logic, the positioning line and the hashtag, #CDA21.

A short hashtag matters for an event. It gives attendees and exhibitors one tag to share under, and it ties the year to the brand so each edition can be told apart.

Because the concept was not implemented, I do not have visuals of a built event or any performance data to share.

How do I approach event concepts?

I start with what the visitor should feel when they walk in, then look for a word or image that holds that feeling.

Events are physical. People move through them. A concept that works on a poster but not in a hall is only half a concept. That is why the gateway idea suited an exhibition: it describes the space as much as the brand.

I have run events on the ground too, including the Magnaite booth at FFCC KSA 2026 in Riyadh, and I wrote about getting leads from exhibitions. For concept work, see creative campaign ideas.

A good event name already describes the room.

Who is this kind of concept work for?

It suits organisers of specialist events who want a name their audience recognises as their own.

Medical, technical and professional exhibitions often compete for the same visitors and the same exhibitors. A concept built on the audience's own vocabulary gives the event a clear identity before a single booth is built.
