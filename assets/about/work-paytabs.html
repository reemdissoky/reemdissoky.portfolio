Creative Campaign Concept · Cairo, Egypt · Proposed — not implemented

Paytabs

Paytabs moves money: sending and receiving payments, with as little friction as possible. A Dubai brand stepping into Egypt, still talking the way it talked at home.

The awareness concept closed that gap with one easy line in everyday Egyptian Arabic, the way people here actually say it: «توصل الفلوس», the money gets there. Simple to remember, simple to repeat.

Agency-side proposal; not built.

Did the Paytabs campaign run?

No. This is a proposed awareness concept that was not implemented.

I developed it agency-side for Paytabs, a Dubai payments brand entering Egypt. What you read here is the reasoning behind the proposal. There is no campaign data, because the campaign was never launched.

I keep it on the site because the line itself is the work, and it shows how I think about language when a brand changes markets.

Why write the slogan in everyday Egyptian Arabic?

Because a payments brand has to sound like it belongs in the market where people spend their money.

Arabic is not one voice. The way a brand speaks in the Gulf is not the way people talk in Cairo. A brand arriving with its home register can sound polished and still feel distant.

Egyptian Arabic is direct, warm and fast. People trust what sounds like them. «توصل الفلوس» is something an Egyptian might say in a normal conversation about sending money. It does not sound like an ad.

That was the point. The line closes the distance between a new brand and an everyday habit.

Why focus on the outcome, not the technology?

Because in payments, the outcome is all the customer really wants to know.

Payment products can be explained in technical terms: gateways, integrations, settlement. For a general awareness campaign, none of that is the message. The message is whether the money arrives.

«توصل الفلوس», the money gets there, answers that question in two words. It is a promise of reliability, said without any claim the audience would need to verify.

Short lines also travel. A line that is easy to remember is easy to repeat, and repetition is how awareness builds.

What makes a market-entry line work?

It has to be simple, local and easy to say out loud.

  • Simple, so it is understood on first hearing.
  • Local, so the brand sounds like it has arrived, not like it is visiting.
  • Easy to say, so people can pass it on.

Those are the three tests I hold any market-entry line to. «توصل الفلوس» passes all three.

How do I approach copy for a new market?

I listen to how people in that market already talk about the problem, then write the brand into that language.

I work in English and Arabic, and I have worked across Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Kuwait. Moving a brand between those markets is mostly a question of voice. The product can stay the same. The words usually cannot.

More on this is on copywriting, creative campaign ideas and digital marketing in Egypt.

Say it the way the market says it.

Who is this kind of work for?

It suits brands moving from one Arab market into another.

A Gulf brand entering Egypt, or an Egyptian brand entering Saudi Arabia, faces the same question: how do we sound local without losing who we are? The answer usually starts with one line written in the market's own words, then a voice built around it.

Keep the brand. Change the accent.

Questions people ask

Was the Paytabs campaign launched?

No. It was an agency-side proposal for Paytabs entering Egypt, and it was not implemented.

What does «توصل الفلوس» mean?

It means "the money gets there", written in simple everyday Egyptian Arabic.

Why not use Modern Standard Arabic for a payments brand?

For an awareness campaign aimed at everyday users in Egypt, the local dialect feels familiar and easy to repeat. Formal Arabic can sound distant in that context.

Can you adapt a Gulf brand's voice for Egypt?

Yes. I have worked in both markets, and adapting voice between them is a core part of my copywriting work.
