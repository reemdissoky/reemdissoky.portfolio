Social Media

Organic strategy and content, plus paid distribution — each platform built for how it's actually used, not one asset resized five ways.

Two real builds anchor this. Taking Magnaite from a single LinkedIn page with about 200 followers to an active presence across seven platforms in one quarter — English, polished, case-study content on LinkedIn and Instagram; Arabic, native-feeling content on X and TikTok, because a platform's actual audience decides the register, not a translation. And Goot Resorts, where the brief came with a hard restriction — no music, on any platform, in an industry where music usually carries the mood. Nature sound, ambient noise, someone talking, humming — never an instrument — and paid spend concentrated on Snapchat, the channel that actually converted, with TikTok added once it earned its share.

What's included in social media work?

Strategy, content, production and paid distribution, run as one plan.

  • Platform strategy. Which platforms, in which language, with what job each one does.
  • Content calendar. Planned around real moments, like course milestones and graduate certifications for Perfect Braces Academy, or National Day for EMAC.
  • Copy and video. Captions in English and Arabic, and video I shoot and direct on the ground.
  • UGC and creator content where it fits, produced with freelancers, as at Goot Resorts.
  • Paid media on Meta, Google and LinkedIn, with budget set against season and sales capacity.

How do you run it?

I set the strategy, write the copy and shoot. A supervised team handles design, media buying and editing.

Every post and every ad set goes through me before it's published. We agree KPIs at the start, so we judge the channels by leads and sales, not by likes alone. When the numbers stall, I look past the ads. At Goot Resorts, that meant sampling sales calls and fixing the handling, which took conversion from 3% to 20% in 6 months.

Who is it for?

  • A brand starting from zero on most platforms.
  • A brand posting regularly without a throughline. Respimar got one: "safe for you, safe for them".
  • A business whose followers grow but whose leads don't.

What do you get first?

A platform plan and a first content calendar, with the KPIs written in.
