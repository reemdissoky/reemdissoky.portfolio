Concept & Naming · Cairo, Egypt

Everybear Agency

Naming and concept creation for an advertising agency built on outdoor-to-concept craft, active since 2006.

What is Everybear Agency?

Everybear is an advertising agency in Cairo, active since 2006.

It specialises in crafting designs from concept all the way to outdoor. That range, from the first idea to a billboard on the street, is what the agency is built around.

What did I do for Everybear?

I did the naming and the concept creation, and I presented the concept.

The mark that came out of it is a polar bear and a moon.

Naming, concept and presentation are one piece of work, not three. The name has to carry the concept, and the presentation has to make both feel obvious to the people who will live with them.

Why is naming an agency harder than naming a product?

Because an agency's name has to sell its own taste.

Every client who hears it is quietly asking: if this is how they name themselves, what will they do for me? A product name can be functional. An agency name is a portfolio piece in one word.

It also has to work on the agency's own output. On a pitch deck. On a signature. Next to a client's logo on a billboard credit. It has to look comfortable in all of them.

How do I approach naming?

I start with what the name needs to do, before I start on what it should sound like.

  • Who has to say it. The team, clients, people who will repeat it in a meeting.
  • Where it has to live. Arabic and English, on screen and on the street.
  • What it has to suggest. The business behind it, without explaining everything.
  • What it has to avoid. Sounding like everyone else in the category.

Only then do the options start. Most don't survive the list.

Why does a concept need a mark to hold it?

Because a name is heard and a mark is seen, and an agency is judged on both.

A strong mark gives the name a shape people can recognise from across a street. For an agency whose work runs all the way to outdoor, that matters more than usual. The mark will sit on large surfaces, at a distance, among a lot of other noise.

A polar bear and a moon is a simple pairing. Simple marks travel well at that scale.

What makes a name last?

A name lasts when it can grow with the business without needing to be explained again.

Agencies change. Services get added, teams change, clients come and go. A name tied too closely to one service or one moment starts to feel small. A name built on an idea and a character has more room.

That is the kind of name I aim for. Something the people inside the business can still be proud of years later, and that a new client can remember after hearing it once.

What does a concept presentation do?

It turns a name from a word on a list into a decision.

Without the story around it, a new name usually sounds strange. A presentation shows how it reads, how it looks, and why it fits. It gives the people who will own the name a reason to choose it with confidence.

Who is this kind of work for?

Businesses at the start of a brand, or ready to rename. Naming, concept and the first presentation, from one person. More in Brand Communication and Creative Campaign Ideas.
