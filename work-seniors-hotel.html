Creative Scriptwriting & Direction · Egypt

Seniors Hotel

Brochure and ad script for a residential-hotel concept blending care and luxury for the elderly, built around comfort and dignity in daily life. Ad creative script and on-set direction, both by Reem.

What is Seniors Hotel?

Seniors Hotel is a residential hotel concept in Egypt that blends care and luxury for the elderly.

It is built to support socio-economic and health initiatives, so residents feel at home. The logo line is "The Elite Care Giver."

That line holds the whole idea. Care, at a standard people would choose for themselves.

What did I do on it?

Three things, from page to set:

  • I wrote the brochure.
  • I wrote the ad script.
  • I directed the ad on set.

Writing and directing the same piece meant the idea didn't have to be handed over halfway through.

Why is this a hard category to write for?

Because it touches one of the most sensitive decisions a family makes.

Choosing where an older parent will live carries love, guilt, worry and relief all at once. Often the decision is shared between the person who will live there and the children who want the best for them. Both read the same brochure with very different feelings.

Copy that sounds clinical pushes people away. Copy that sounds like a sales pitch makes them suspicious. The writing has to feel like a place you would trust with someone you love.

How do care and luxury sit together?

They sit together through dignity.

Care on its own can sound like loss: of independence, of home, of routine. Luxury on its own can sound distant. Put together around comfort and dignity in daily life, they describe something people actually want for their parents.

That balance is what I wrote toward, in the brochure and in the script.

What does the brochure have to do that the ad can't?

It has to answer questions in the reader's own time.

An ad opens the door. It sets the feeling in a few seconds and moves on. A brochure is read slowly, often more than once, often by several people in the same family.

So a brochure in this category has to carry the detail an ad leaves out. What daily life looks like. What care means in practice. Why the place can be trusted. It has to keep the same warmth as the ad while giving people enough to discuss at the kitchen table.

That is why I treat the ad and the brochure as a pair. The ad makes people want to know more. The brochure is where they find it.

What does directing on set add?

It keeps the tone the script was written in.

A script about dignity can lose it in a single shot. The pace of a scene, how a resident is framed, how close the camera gets. These are small choices, and they decide whether the ad feels respectful.

Being on set as the writer and director meant I could make those calls in the moment, against the intent of the words.

Who is this kind of work for?

Brands in sensitive categories, where the wrong tone costs more than the wrong colour. Healthcare, care services, family decisions.

It also suits brands launching a concept that doesn't exist yet in people's minds. When there is no familiar category to lean on, the words have to explain the idea and make people feel it at the same time.
