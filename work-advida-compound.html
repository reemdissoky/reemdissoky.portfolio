Real Estate Development · Cairo West, Egypt

ADVIDA Compound

SEO and digital brand strategy for a sustainable real estate developer's flagship 5-acre project in New Zayed, introduced by founder Ahmed El-Shennawy.

Who is behind ADVIDA?

ADVIDA is a project by ADVA, a sustainable real estate developer working since 2019.

ADVA was founded by Ahmed El-Shennawy and builds on his 11 years of experience in real estate. The company describes its work in three parts: integrated cities, luxury properties, and real communities.

ADVIDA is a 5-acre project in New Zayed, in West Cairo. It was built in collaboration with ADVA's sister companies in construction and interior design.

That last detail matters for the story. One group covering the build and the interiors is a promise a buyer can understand without a sales pitch.

What did I work on?

Three things: SEO, the digital brand strategy, and the brochure.

  • SEO. Making sure the project can be found by people already searching for homes in its area.
  • Digital brand strategy. How ADVIDA presents itself online, and how that connects back to ADVA as the developer.
  • The brochure. The piece a buyer takes home and reads slowly.

Each one answers a different moment in the same decision.

Why does SEO matter for a single compound?

Because buyers in West Cairo compare a lot of projects before they ever call a sales office. Much of that comparing happens in search.

New Zayed sits in one of the most competitive residential markets around Cairo. Many developers are selling into the same area to the same families. A project that doesn't show up when someone searches for its area, its size, or its developer is relying on ads alone to be seen.

My approach to real estate SEO is simple. Find the questions buyers type. Answer them on pages built for the project. Keep the developer's name and the project's name tied together, so a search for one surfaces the other.

More on this in SEO.

How does a developer story carry into a project brand?

It carries through the reasons a buyer should trust it.

A younger developer has to earn trust differently from a decades-old name. ADVA's founder brings real estate experience that predates the company, and the group covers construction and interiors through its own companies. Those are facts a buyer can weigh.

My approach in digital brand strategy is to put those facts where they do the most work. Not as a list of claims, but as the reason the project looks and feels the way it does.

Why still make a brochure?

Because property is a family decision, and families pass things around.

A brochure is the version of the project that sits on a table between conversations. It has to hold the same story as the website, in a form that works offline and in someone else's hands.

The digital work and the print work need to say the same thing. That was the point of doing both.

Who is this kind of work for?

Developers launching a specific project who want search, brand and print handled by one person, from one strategy.

When those three are split between different suppliers, the project usually ends up described three different ways. A buyer notices. Keeping them together means the brochure, the website and the search results all point to the same reasons to buy.

It suits a developer with a real story to tell and no time to brief three teams on it. I take the strategy, write the words, and keep the supporting design work on track against what we agreed up front.
