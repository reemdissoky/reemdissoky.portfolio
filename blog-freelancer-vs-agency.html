SMEs

Freelance marketer or agency? How SMEs in Saudi Arabia and Egypt should decide.

It's the wrong first question. The right one is: who is going to own the plan?

By Reem Dissoky · Published 30 October 2026 · Updated 30 October 2026

Should a small business hire a freelance marketer or an agency?

Hire an agency when you need volume and you already know the plan. Hire a senior freelance strategist when you need someone to make the plan and answer for it. Hire in-house when marketing is a daily, permanent job.

Most SMEs don't fail at marketing because they picked the wrong vendor. They fail because nobody owned the strategy. The agency waited for a brief. The freelancer waited for direction. The owner was busy running the business.

When does an agency make sense?

  • You already know your positioning, your audience and your numbers.
  • You need a lot of output across many channels, every month.
  • You have someone internal who can brief them well and check the work.

That last point is the one people skip. An agency is only as good as the brief it gets.

When does a freelancer make sense?

"Freelancer" covers two very different people.

The specialist does one thing: a designer, a media buyer, a video editor. Great when you know exactly what you need made.

The strategist decides what should be made at all, and why. Then either makes it, or makes sure it gets made right. That's closer to a fractional marketing manager than to a freelancer.

Hiring three specialists without a strategist gives you three people waiting for a brief.

What about hiring in-house?

Worth it when marketing is a full-time, permanent job inside the business, and you can offer someone a real career there. In Riyadh and Cairo, a strong senior marketer is a significant hire. Make sure you know what you're hiring them to do before you write the job post.

Five questions to ask before you decide

  1. Who owns the strategy? If the answer is "we'll figure it out", you need a strategist first.
  2. What's the one number this has to move? Leads, bookings, applications, signed deals.
  3. Who checks quality before things go live? Someone has to say no.
  4. What happens after the lead? If sales or CRM is where deals die, more ads won't fix it.
  5. Which languages does your customer read in? In Saudi Arabia and Egypt, usually two. Translated copy shows.

What I offer instead

I sit in the middle on purpose. I build the strategy myself, run my own specialties hands-on, and supervise a supporting team when the work needs more hands. You get one accountable person, not an agency structure. Here is how that works.

The honest limit: there's one of me. If you need forty campaigns a month, hire the agency, and hire someone to brief it.

How do you brief whoever you hire?

With one page that answers the five questions above before the first meeting.

  1. Write down the number. One business number, where it is today, and where you need it to be.
  2. Name the owner. Who on your side makes decisions, and who signs off on quality.
  3. Share what already happened. Past campaigns, what worked, what didn't, and what the sales team hears from customers.
  4. Agree the KPIs together. Before any work starts, not after the first report.

A strategist, an agency or an in-house hire will all do better work with that page. If you can't fill it in yet, that tells you who to hire first.

Questions people ask

Is a freelance marketer cheaper than an agency?

Usually, per month. But cheaper only matters if the work moves a number. A cheap freelancer without a strategy can cost more than an agency with one.

What is a fractional marketing manager?

A senior marketer who leads your marketing part-time: owns the strategy, sets KPIs and manages the work, without being a full-time employee.

Can one person really run marketing for an SME?

One person can own it. They shouldn't do every task alone. The model that works is one accountable strategist with specialists under their supervision.
