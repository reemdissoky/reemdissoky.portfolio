Creative Campaign Ideas

The concept a campaign is actually built around, not just its execution. Two examples, in the room they were built for:

The Magnaite Connect wall. The idea was to make an abstract product — "one system running every branch" — physically legible in three seconds. A single cloud icon sits at the center of the wall, with lines running out to what it actually replaces: a live dashboard, remote access, POS integration on one side; plate recognition, AI alerts, delivery-aggregator intelligence on the other. Anyone walking past reads the whole pitch without a single conversation.

Find Your Business Match. For BiznEX, the problem was never getting brands and investors in the same room — it was matching the right ones fast enough to matter. Borrowing the dating-app mechanic reframed the whole expo as a deliberate pairing process rather than a crowd to wade through, and it carried through signage, content, and on-site experience as one conceit, not a slogan bolted onto separate assets.

More concept work — eLiver ("eLiver can deliver, even a dot" — a delivery brand built on the emotion of getting a package, not its logistics), Cairo Dental Arch (a dental exhibition's identity built from an architectural gateway and a dental term, at once), and more — see the full list on the Work page.

What's included in a campaign idea?

The central idea, and enough of its execution to prove it works across every place the campaign will run.

  • The audience thesis. Who we're talking to and the moment the idea meets them. At FFCC KSA 2026 it was multi-branch F&B owners and "one slow second" at the counter or drive-thru.
  • The idea. One concept, written in a sentence, that the whole campaign hangs on.
  • The line. A slogan or headline in English, Arabic, or both.
  • How it travels. Sketches of how the idea shows up on social, in ads, on a booth wall, on signage or in a video script.
  • A concept presentation. So the people who approve it can see the whole thing at once, as I did for Everybear Agency.

How do you develop the idea?

I come up with the concept myself, then a supervised team helps produce it.

I start with the audience, not the product. The question is what the audience already feels, and how the brand can meet that feeling. eLiver is built on the emotion of receiving a package, not the logistics behind it. Ayla Kitchen took its name from the Arabic word for family and built a playful, pun-driven campaign around it: "One Bite, Habibi!"

Some ideas go through rounds. eLiver's name and logotype took several before landing on the dot as the delivery drop.

Once the idea is approved, the team works on design and production. I check every piece against the concept before it goes out, so the idea stays one idea.

Who needs a campaign idea?

It suits moments when being remembered matters more than being seen.

  • A launch, or a brand entering a new market. The Paytabs proposal (proposed, not implemented) was an awareness idea for a Dubai payments brand entering Egypt.
  • An event or exhibition that needs to stand apart from the others in its category, like the Cairo Dental Arch concept (proposed, not implemented).
  • A brand with plenty of content and no single thread running through it.

What do you get first?

A short concept presentation: the audience thesis, the idea, the line, and how it would look in two or three places.

You decide on the idea before anyone spends on production.

Questions people ask

Can you develop a campaign idea without running the campaign?

Yes. The concept can be the whole engagement. I can also stay on and supervise production.

Do you create ideas for events and exhibitions?

Yes. BiznEX, the Egypt Retail Summit and Magnaite at FFCC KSA 2026 were all event work, and at FFCC I built the booth from concept to installation.

Do your ideas work in Arabic?

They're built to. "Find Your Business Match" worked in both languages because the mechanic is universal, not because the words translate.
