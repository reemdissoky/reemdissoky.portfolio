GEO · SEO

GEO vs SEO: what changes for a small business.

Your customers used to search, scroll and click. Now a lot of them ask, and read one answer. Here is what that changes, and what it doesn't.

By Reem Dissoky · Published 30 October 2026 · Updated 30 October 2026

What is the difference between GEO and SEO?

SEO earns your page a place in a list of results. GEO earns your brand a mention inside the answer an AI assistant writes. Same foundations. Different finish line.

With search, the user does the last step. They compare ten links and choose. With ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overviews, the engine does that step for them. It reads, decides, and writes a paragraph. Sometimes it names three companies. Sometimes one. Sometimes none of them is you.

What stays the same?

More than people think. The engines still need to reach your pages, read them, and trust them. So the boring work still matters:

  • Pages that load fast and render without waiting on JavaScript.
  • One clear topic per page, with a real heading structure.
  • Content written for the person asking, not for a keyword tool.
  • Other sites that mention you, and agree with what you say about yourself.

If your SEO is broken, your GEO will be too. GEO is a layer on top, not a replacement.

What changes with GEO?

Consistency beats volume.

An assistant builds its answer from everything it can find about you. If your website says one thing, your LinkedIn says another, and an old directory listing says a third, the answer comes out vague. Or wrong. One entity statement, repeated word for word, does more than ten new blog posts.

Answers beat articles.

Engines pull clean, self-contained sentences. A heading phrased as the question. The answer in the first line under it. A list when there are steps. That's why this post is built the way it is.

Structure beats guessing.

Schema markup (Organization, Person, Service, FAQ) tells the machine directly what you are, where you work and what you offer. Without it, the engine guesses from your prose.

Prompts beat rankings.

You can't measure GEO with a rank tracker. You measure it by asking the engines the questions your buyers ask, every month, and writing down what comes back.

Five things a small business should fix first

  1. Ask the engines about yourself. Ask who your brand is, then ask for the best provider of your service in your city. Save the answers.
  2. Write your entity statement. One sentence: who, what, where, for whom. Put it in the first lines of your homepage.
  3. Make every profile match it. LinkedIn, Google Business Profile, Instagram bio, directories.
  4. Add schema. At minimum Organization (or Person) with links to those profiles.
  5. Rewrite your service pages as answers. What is it, who is it for, what does it cost to start, what happens first.

None of this needs a big budget. It needs someone to sit down and make the facts agree with each other.

If you want that someone to be me: GEO.

Questions people ask

Is GEO the same as AEO?

Close enough for most businesses. AEO (answer engine optimization) and GEO both describe optimizing for AI-written answers instead of ranked links. The work is the same: clear entity facts, structured data, answer-shaped content.

Do I still need SEO if I do GEO?

Yes. AI engines rely on the same crawlable, well-structured, trusted pages that SEO builds. GEO without SEO has nothing to stand on.

How do I measure GEO?

Pick ten to twenty questions your buyers ask. Ask them in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google every month. Track whether you are named, and whether the facts are right.
